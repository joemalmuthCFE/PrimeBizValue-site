Will this deal clear debt service — before you spend $1,500 and three weeks finding out?

SBA 7(a) acquisition lending is busy: 7,003 acquisition loans in FY2025, $8.29 billion, up 34.6% year over year. The process has a specific and expensive shape for a first-time borrower. You find a business, you apply, the lender underwrites, and somewhere in there a valuation you did not order and cannot accelerate arrives and either supports the price or does not. This page is about the question you are allowed to answer yourself, in advance.

The consequence nobody selling you a valuation will volunteer. Because the lender engages the appraiser, no valuation firm can sell a borrower an early answer — the only compliant report is one you are not allowed to commission. So the borrower faces a near-certain $1,500–$5,000, one-to-three-week determination they cannot start, cannot rush, and cannot see coming. That gap is not a marketing position we invented; it is created by the regulation itself. The one thing you are permitted to buy for yourself is the arithmetic in advance.

The screen: what it actually calculates

Debt service coverage is not mysterious. It is one subtraction and one division, and the entire art is in being honest about the inputs. Worked on an illustrative deal:

Illustrative only · not a customer, not a real transaction

Step 1 — the money the business actually generates for a new owner.

Defensible SDE, after striking the add-backs that cannot be evidenced$310,000
Less a market salary for whoever runs the business — you, or your manager−$95,000
Less an annual capital replacement reserve (vehicles, equipment, technology)−$18,000
Cash available for debt service$197,000

Two lines decide most outcomes. Sellers and their packages routinely present SDE with no owner salary deducted, because SDE is defined that way — but you cannot eat a definition, and a lender will not underwrite one. If you need $130,000 to live on rather than $95,000, the whole screen moves.

What a screen like this is for. Not to predict the decision. To tell you which of three situations you are in: the price has room, the price is at the edge, or the price cannot be financed at all and you are about to spend weeks and fees discovering that. The third case is the one worth $799 of anybody's money.

Where the screen bites hardest

  • SDE under roughly $250,000. Once a market salary and a capital reserve come out, there is not much left to divide.
  • An owner who needs a large salary. Coverage is calculated after you eat, not before.
  • Short amortisation. Goodwill-heavy deals amortised over ten years behave very differently from real-estate-backed deals over twenty-five.
  • Add-backs that will not survive. Every undocumented adjustment in the seller's package is earnings the lender removes and you were counting on. The recast worksheet in the report flags these explicitly, which is the same reason it is useful before you sign an LOI.
  • A seller note that is not truly on standby. If it amortises alongside the bank loan, it is debt service too.

Questions people actually ask

Is this an SBA-compliant business valuation?

No. It is not, we will never describe it as one, and you should not put it in a loan file as one. Under SOP 50 10 8, effective 1 June 2025, an independent business valuation must be ordered by the lender - not by you - from an appraiser holding an ASA, CBA, ABV, CVA, AVA, or BCA credential who is independent of loan production. You cannot buy that report from us or from anyone else, because you are not permitted to be the one who engages the appraiser. What we sell is a pre-application screen you run before any of that starts.

When does the SBA require an independent business valuation?

Under SOP 50 10 8, when the financed intangible or goodwill portion of the transaction exceeds $250,000, or when the buyer and seller are related parties - in which case it is required regardless of amount. Below $250,000 of financed intangibles and with unrelated parties, the lender may generally perform the valuation in-house. Your lender applies the rule; confirm the specifics with them.

What does an SBA business valuation cost and how long does it take?

Independent valuations obtained for SBA acquisition financing commonly run about $1,500 to $5,000 and take one to three weeks. A full credentialed narrative appraisal for court or tax purposes typically runs $3,000 to $15,000. The cost and the calendar are the reason a pre-application screen exists: it is worth knowing whether the deal clears debt service before you spend either.

How much money do I need to put down on an SBA 7(a) acquisition loan?

SOP 50 10 8 sets a minimum equity injection of 10% of the total project cost for a complete change of ownership. A portion can, in some structures, be met with seller debt on full standby - but that is a lender-specific determination and you should ask your lender how they treat it rather than assume.

What debt service coverage ratio do I need?

The SBA does not publish one universal borrower-facing number; lenders set their own thresholds, and 1.25x is a level commonly applied in acquisition underwriting. Ask your lender directly what they require, on what earnings definition, and after what owner compensation - the definition matters as much as the ratio.

Can I buy part of a business with an SBA 7(a) loan?

Yes, but the structure is constrained. Under SOP 50 10 8 a partial change of ownership - one partner buying out another, for example - must be structured as a stock or membership-interest purchase rather than an asset purchase, because the entity continues. That has real tax and liability consequences and it is a conversation for your lawyer and CPA before you sign anything.
