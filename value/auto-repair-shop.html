How much is an auto repair shop worth?

Auto repair shops are valued on transferable cash flow, and the central question is whether the customers belong to the shop or to the owner behind the counter. Whether the real estate is included changes the deal structure more than anything else.

What multiple do auto repair shops sell for?

Main Street businesses are priced as a multiple of Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE) — net profit plus the owner's salary, perks, depreciation, interest, and one-time costs added back. The multiple applied depends primarily on the size of that cash flow, not on the industry label:

What actually changes what an auto repair shop is worth

Real estate: owned, leased, or bundled

Many shops sit on owner-occupied property. A market-rate rent has to be charged against earnings before any cash-flow multiple is meaningful, and the property is then valued as its own asset.

Bay count and utilisation

Value scales with billable bay hours, not square footage. A shop running near capacity with room to add a bay is worth more than one with idle lifts and nobody to run them.

Technician bench and certification

Technicians are the binding constraint in this trade. Certified techs on stable pay plans who intend to stay make the business transferable; a one-man shop where the owner turns the wrenches largely does not.

Diagnostic equipment and OEM subscriptions

Late-model, ADAS, and EV work requires current scan tools, manufacturer subscriptions, and training. A shop that skipped that investment has a shrinking addressable market and the buyer prices the catch-up cost.

Car count, repeat customers, and inspection process

Repeat-customer percentage, average repair order, and a documented digital vehicle inspection process are the closest thing this trade has to recurring revenue.

Environmental and site condition

Waste oil, solvents, old underground tanks, and lifts with expired inspections all surface in diligence. Unresolved environmental exposure is a common reason a repair-shop deal dies at the lender.

Add-backs that typically apply to an auto repair shop

Recasting is where most of the value is won or lost. These are the adjustments a buyer or broker will normally accept — and each one needs to be documented, because a buyer will challenge anything that isn't:

  • Owner's compensation and payroll taxes
  • Owner's personal vehicles and fuel
  • Family payroll above market
  • Above-market rent paid to an owner-related entity
  • One-time equipment purchases expensed
  • Depreciation on lifts and equipment
  • Interest expense
If you own both the building and the business, a buyer will normalise rent to market before applying a multiple. Our report separates the two so you can see what the operation is worth on its own and negotiate the property as a distinct asset.

How much can I sell my auto repair shop for?

The honest answer is a range, not a number — and the range narrows once your add-backs are documented. Businesses sold in 2025 achieved 94% of asking price on average and took 169–207 days to sell (BizBuySell). Pricing correctly at the start matters more than negotiating hard later.

Two things move the number in your favour before you list:

  • A documented recast. Undocumented add-backs get stripped out by the buyer's accountant. Documented ones survive.
  • Financing feasibility. If the deal doesn't clear a lender's debt-service coverage test at your asking price, your realistic buyer pool shrinks to cash buyers — who pay less.
