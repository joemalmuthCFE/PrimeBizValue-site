How much is an HVAC business worth?

HVAC companies are among the most sought-after Main Street businesses, and the spread between a well-run one and an owner-dependent one is wide. Recurring maintenance agreements are usually the single largest value driver.

What multiple do HVAC companies sell for?

Main Street businesses are priced as a multiple of Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE) — net profit plus the owner's salary, perks, depreciation, interest, and one-time costs added back. The multiple applied depends primarily on the size of that cash flow, not on the industry label:

What actually changes what an HVAC business is worth

Maintenance agreement base

Recurring service contracts are the closest thing to predictable revenue in the trades. A large, transferable agreement base commands a premium over a purely break-fix book.

Owner dependency

If the owner is the top technician, the estimator, and the relationship, a buyer is purchasing a job. Owner-dependent businesses discount heavily.

Licensed staff who will stay

The buyer needs licensure. Whether the license travels with the owner or with employed technicians materially changes the risk.

Replacement vs. new construction mix

New-construction-weighted revenue is more cyclical and generally valued more conservatively than replacement and service work.

Customer concentration

Builder or property-management concentration is a risk factor a buyer will price in.

Add-backs that typically apply to an HVAC business

Recasting is where most of the value is won or lost. These are the adjustments a buyer or broker will normally accept — and each one needs to be documented, because a buyer will challenge anything that isn't:

  • Owner's compensation above market rate
  • Owner's vehicle and fuel
  • Family members on payroll above market
  • One-time legal or settlement costs
  • Depreciation on trucks and equipment
  • Interest expense

How much can I sell my HVAC business for?

The honest answer is a range, not a number — and the range narrows once your add-backs are documented. Businesses sold in 2025 achieved 94% of asking price on average and took 169–207 days to sell (BizBuySell). Pricing correctly at the start matters more than negotiating hard later.

Two things move the number in your favour before you list:

  • A documented recast. Undocumented add-backs get stripped out by the buyer's accountant. Documented ones survive.
  • Financing feasibility. If the deal doesn't clear a lender's debt-service coverage test at your asking price, your realistic buyer pool shrinks to cash buyers — who pay less.
