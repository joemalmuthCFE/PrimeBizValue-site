Everything people usually want to know before they submit their numbers.

What is SDE, and how is it different from EBITDA?

Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE) is the standard way small, owner-operated businesses are valued. It starts with your business's net income, then adds back the owner's salary, personal perks run through the business, depreciation, interest, and other one-time or discretionary costs — arriving at the true cash benefit available to a single owner-operator. Adjusted EBITDA is used instead for larger businesses that already have a management team in place, since a buyer in that situation isn't stepping in to do the owner's job themselves.

Is this a certified appraisal or an official business valuation?

No. This is an informational, directional estimate — not a certified appraisal, formal valuation, or fairness opinion, and it hasn't been reviewed by a CPA, licensed business appraiser, or lender. It's built to give you a real, defensible starting number using the same methodology brokers use, but for an SBA loan application, a formal sale, or a legal matter, you'll want a credentialed appraiser to sign off.

How is the valuation multiple determined?

We use four fixed Main Street SDE-size tiers, based on published industry transaction data (BizBuySell, IBBA/M&A Source Market Pulse), and the tool automatically selects the tier that matches your business's calculated SDE. From there, adjustment factors for earnings trend, risk, and upside move the multiple within a realistic range for your specific situation.

How accurate is the estimate?

As accurate as the financial information you provide and the assumptions that go into any rule-of-thumb, multiple-based valuation. It's the same core methodology a business broker uses for an informal opinion of value — genuinely useful for planning, negotiating, or sanity-checking an asking price, but it isn't a substitute for a professional appraisal when real money and legal paperwork are on the line.

Who is this for?

Owners thinking about selling, buyers evaluating an asking price, anyone heading into an SBA financing conversation who wants to know if a deal actually services the debt, and franchisors who want their franchisees walking into resale conversations with real numbers instead of guesses.

My business doesn't fit the "Main Street" size range — can I still use this?

The tool is built and calibrated specifically for businesses valued under about $2M — the range where SDE-based, broker-style valuation is the standard approach. If your business is meaningfully larger, an EBITDA-based valuation from a lower-middle-market advisor will generally serve you better than this tool.
