What a mispriced resale actually costs you

Every franchise development leader knows this list. It's worth seeing it written down, because the cost lands on the brand, not on the seller who set the price.

Deals die in underwriting

An asking price the cash flow can't service doesn't fail at the offer stage. It fails weeks later at the lender, after everyone has spent time and legal fees on it.

Stale listings become your comps

An overpriced unit sitting on the market for nine months teaches every other owner in your system what their store is "worth." One bad number poisons the whole conversation.

Units go dark instead of transferring

A seller who can't get their number often stops trying. The store closes, the territory opens, and you are back to selling a new franchise instead of transferring a running one.

Your team becomes the appraiser

Somebody on your side ends up building a valuation in a spreadsheet, for free, under pressure, with no methodology to point at when the seller disagrees.

What your franchisees get

The same report, run the same way, every time. That consistency is the point: when two owners in your system get a number, the numbers were produced by identical logic, and neither one can argue that the other got special treatment.

  • A documented recast. Add-backs applied by a consistent process, not ad hoc judgment that shifts from deal to deal.
  • A benchmarked multiple. Drawn from fixed SDE-size tiers calibrated against published transaction data, not a number somebody liked.
  • An SBA reality check. Buyer financing and DSCR run before the deal goes to a lender, so the asking price is tested against the debt it has to service.
  • A closing cash-to-seller breakdown. What the owner actually walks away with, which is the number they care about and rarely see until it's too late.
  • A "presented by" line. The report carries your brand's name where it's prepared and presented, so it arrives as part of your resale process rather than as a random website.

How the partner program works

  1. We issue your brand a code

    A unique discount code tied to your system. It runs indefinitely until you ask us to switch it off.

  2. You put it in front of your owners

    Resale packet, intranet, transfer checklist, exit conversation, annual meeting. Wherever your sellers already look. No portal to build, no system to integrate. Or skip the code entirely and send them a branded page of your own, which is covered further down.

  3. Your franchisees get a discounted report

    They upload a P&L, the tool recasts it, and the report comes back on screen and by email. Ten minutes, not three weeks.

  4. We report activity under your code

    Every order placed under your brand's code is tracked separately, so you can see the volume your system is generating instead of guessing at it.

  5. You get paid quarterly

    A statement of every order under your code, and a payment for your share of it. W-9 on file, ACH or check, no minimum to hit before you see money.

A 500-unit system sees roughly 20 transfers a year at the industry rate. On a realistic mix of tiers, that's somewhere between $800 and $3,200 back to the brand annually. A 5,000-unit platform lands closer to $9,000 to $36,000.

Let's be straight about what that is: it will not move your P&L. What it does is pay for the resale program that produces it, which turns a cost center into something near break-even. The real return is the transfers that close instead of stalling, and the units that stay open paying royalties.

Questions we usually get

Is this an appraisal?

No, and we say so plainly on every report. This is an opinion of value built on a documented, repeatable methodology. It is designed to get a seller to a defensible starting number and to test that number against what a lender will actually finance. It is not a certified appraisal and does not replace one where a formal appraisal is required.

What happens to our franchisees' financial data?

It's retained for the purpose of producing and supporting the report, and it is not sold or shared externally beyond the service providers needed to run the service. Every user accepts that commitment before uploading anything. The full policy is on our Confidentiality and Privacy Policy pages.

Do you give our brand the underlying data?

We report activity under your code, not your franchisees' individual financials. If you want unit-level reporting, that's a conversation to have with your owners first, not something we'll hand over by default.

Are you going to broker our resales?

No. We produce the number and stop there. We don't list units, we don't take a commission on a transfer, and we have no stake in whether a deal closes. That independence is the reason the number is worth anything in a negotiation.

What does it cost the brand to set up?

Nothing. There's no partner fee, no minimum volume, no contract term, and no technical integration. If it isn't producing value for your system, tell us and we'll switch the code off.

How do we handle this in our FDD?

Most brands treat a partner share like any other supplier rebate and disclose it in Item 8, alongside the other revenue the franchisor receives in connection with franchisee purchases. We're not your counsel and we won't pretend to be, so run the structure past your franchise attorney and tell us how they want it papered. If the disclosure is a problem for your system, we'll happily run your code as a pure franchisee discount with no share back to the brand.

How and when do you pay?

Quarterly, on a statement listing every order placed under your code with the amount collected and your share of each. Payment by ACH or check once we have a W-9 on file. There's no minimum to clear before you get paid, and no expiration on the code.
